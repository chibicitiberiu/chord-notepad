- Desktop: the app is completely redesigned. Every part of the window is new, built from scratch with Qt Quick: a modern look of its own, the same on Linux, Windows and macOS, in a light or a dark theme (Settings › General, ☰ › Dark theme or Ctrl+Shift+L; System follows your desktop), with the Inter font, crisp at any display scaling, and a window you can zoom as a whole (Ctrl+Alt+Plus, Ctrl+Alt+Minus). The old Tk window is gone. Downloads are about 30 MB bigger because they carry Qt, and macOS now needs 14.4 or newer.
- Desktop: two workspaces. **Edit** (Ctrl+1) has the toolbar and the rail with the Directives palette and the Chord identifier; **Play** (Ctrl+2) has the player bar, the frozen text and the chord sheet, always shown. In Edit, F9 shows or hides the chord sheet.
- Desktop: the menus moved to a ☰ button. The File, Edit, View, Playback, Tools and Help menu bar is still there: ☰ › Show menu bar keeps it, tapping Alt shows it for a moment, and Alt+F, Alt+E and so on open a menu. Every command is in the menus, including the editor's line operations, Change value… and the chord sheet's zoom.
- Desktop: the player bar in Play has a timeline with a segment for each labelled section (click or drag to seek), the bar and beat, a speed popup (slider, box and presets), a volume popup with mute and a separate Metronome slider, and buttons for the voicing and the instrument. Tempo, key and time signature sit centred above the timeline; click one the song sets with a directive to jump to that directive. At a speed other than 100% the tempo shows what you hear too: "143 bpm (150%)".
- Desktop: the tempo picker (▾ next to a tempo) has a Tap button: tap along with the song and it finds the bpm.
- Desktop: a playhead, as on Android. Click a chord to hear it and move the marker there; ▶ plays from the marker, Shift+▶ from the text cursor, and a click while a song plays jumps playback to that chord. ⏮ keeps playing if the song was playing, and ■ goes back to the first chord. Bars are counted from the top of the song. A click in the chord sheet or the timeline scrolls the text to that chord.
- Desktop: chords in the text work like links. Every click plays the chord, also several quick clicks in a row; Shift+click plays it rolled and keeps your selection; Alt+click, Alt+double-click and Alt+drag select the chord's text instead.
- Desktop: the Insert dialogs are gone. The Directives palette inserts a tempo, time, key, capo, label, loop, rest or comment with its value selected (click a tile or drag it into the text), and a ▾ next to a directive opens a picker for its value. The Edit toolbar has a Suggest capo button, and the Transpose and Capo tooltips say what they do with a selection.
- Desktop: the Chord identifier is a sidebar. Click a result to hear it (Shift+click rolled), double-click it or drag it into the text to insert it. The roll is slower, 0.11 s between notes, here and wherever a chord is rolled.
- Desktop: the editor does what you expect from a text editor. Find and Replace in a bar under the text (Ctrl+F, Ctrl+H, F3 and Shift+F3), Go to line (Ctrl+G), duplicate, move and delete lines (Ctrl+D, Ctrl+Shift+Up/Down, Ctrl+Shift+K), select the line (Ctrl+L), comment lines (Ctrl+/), indent with Tab and Shift+Tab, and overwrite mode (Insert). Page Up and Page Down work, End over a selection goes to its end, Ctrl+Enter types a line break, and the other occurrences of a selected word get a light tint. The right-click menu has the common operations, and Ctrl+wheel zooms the text.
- Desktop: Settings is a window with Save and Discard. A number outside its range is marked, and its tooltip gives the range. The font is in Settings › General, and so is the new Theme. The audio driver has an Auto entry.
- Desktop: Settings › Voicings lists the built-in voicings too, read only, with a button to copy one. Add voicing opens a menu: a blank fretboard, piano or ensemble voicing with valid defaults, or a copy of any voicing, named "New <name>". The trash button next to it deletes the selected voicing.
- Desktop: the status bar shows what is playing, the bar, tempo, time signature and key in fixed cells, and the line and column. "Read only while playing" appears only when you try to edit during playback.
- Desktop: shortcuts that were missing or wrong now work: Undo and Redo are greyed out when there is nothing to undo, Ctrl+Y and Ctrl+Shift+Z both redo, Ctrl+O no longer types a line break, shortcuts work with Caps Lock on and don't fire behind a dialog, every dialog closes with Escape, a time signature you type is applied, and the tempo wheel no longer sticks at multiples of 10. New: F5 and Shift+F5, Ctrl+M, Ctrl+] and Ctrl+[ for the speed, Ctrl+T, Ctrl+,, F11 and Alt+1 to Alt+4 for the chord sheet's views.
- Desktop: the chord sheet scrolls when you drag it, zooms with Ctrl+wheel in every view (the piano roll too), and has a zoom control like the editor's. Shift+click rolls a chord. The playing chord is marked with an accent tint over it. A time signature set in the player bar redraws the sheet at once.
- Desktop: the Quick Start keeps one size on every page, and About's GitHub link opens in your browser.
- Desktop: the user guide and the Quick Start describe the new window, with new screenshots in light and dark. The guide has a dark mode that follows your browser or system, and the Quick Start's picture follows the app's theme.
- Chord sheet: every second section is on a slightly darker paper, so sections are easy to tell apart.
- Piano roll: redrawn. Notes are rounded bars with their names in them (in your notation, spelled for the key), the x axis is time with a bar and beat grid (C*8 in 4/4 takes two bars), the grid is quieter, and a chord name that doesn't fit is drawn smaller instead of hidden. The roll can be zoomed.
- Staff view: one continuous staff from the clefs on, every chord name at one size on one baseline (no more staircase), a final bar line at the end of the song, and repeat signs around looped passes. The voice legend lists the voices from high to low (soprano, alto, tenor, bass). On a tall sheet the notes stay at a readable size. On the desktop a notice says that every chord is drawn as a whole note; Dismiss hides it.
- Fret cards: a rest gets a card the size of the chords' cards.
- Ensembles: a chord next to the same chord over another bass (C then C/E) no longer sounds the same; the voicer prefers a different voicing for the second one. The weight is "Same chord, same bass" in the voicing form, −8 by default.
- Click to hear: a roman numeral (I, IV, V7) sounds in the key the song sets above it, not in C.
- Android: Stop leaves the playhead on the chord's first pass again, and the piano roll's keyboard has dark keys in the dark theme.
